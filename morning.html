Social Security checks will go up 3.6% in January

The Social Security Administration announced yesterday that benefits will rise 3.6% next year. For the average retiree, that's about $75 more a month, and it's the biggest raise in four years. The increase is tied to how much prices went up over the past year.

What this means for you

If you get Social Security, the raise shows up on its own in your January payment. There's nothing to sign up for. You'll get a notice in early December with your exact new amount.

One thing to keep in mind: Medicare hasn't announced next year's Part B premium yet, and that usually comes out of the same check, so a little of the raise may go toward it.

Election Day is 19 days away

Kentucky voters will choose a new U.S. senator this year, since Mitch McConnell is retiring, along with members of Congress and all 100 seats in the state House. Campaigns are picking up, and President Trump will hold a rally for Republican candidates in Highland Heights on Saturday.

Why you're seeing this: you're on Medicare

Medicare open enrollment starts today

From now until December 7, people on Medicare can switch plans for next year. Medicare expects average Advantage plan premiums to go down, and the average stand-alone drug plan to go up by less than a dollar a month. Averages hide a lot, though. Some drug plans are raising premiums by $50 or more.

What this means for you

If you're happy with your coverage, you don't have to do anything. It's still worth opening the "Annual Notice of Change" letter from your plan and checking two things: your premium, and whether your doctors and prescriptions are still covered.

You'll likely see a lot of Medicare ads and calls over the next few weeks. You don't have to answer any of them. For free, unbiased help, Kentucky's SHIP counselors can walk you through your options.

Measles in Kentucky

What changed since last time

Kentucky has now reported 157 cases this year, after more than 50 new cases were confirmed Tuesday, many tied to a few large households in Laurel County. It's the state's worst year for measles since 1991.

The short backstory: the outbreak started in western Kentucky this summer, and nearly every case has been in someone who wasn't vaccinated.

What this means for you

If you're not sure whether you've had your measles shots, your doctor or pharmacist can check for you.
